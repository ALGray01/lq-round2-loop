The agreement auto-renews yearly unless either side gives 60 days' written notice before the renewal date — miss that window and you're locked in for another year. Either side can also terminate early for "material breach," but only after a 30-day cure period, except for non-payment, which allows immediate termination.

9.1 This Agreement commences on the Effective Date and continues for an initial term of twelve (12) months, automatically renewing for successive twelve (12) month periods unless either party gives the other not less than sixty (60) days' written notice prior to the end of the then-current term. 9.2 Either party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other party fails to pay any undisputed amount within thirty (30) days of its due date. 9.3 Either party may terminate for any other material breach not cured within thirty (30) days of written notice specifying the breach.

Liability is capped at fees paid in the preceding 12 months — for a new engagement, that could be a small number relative to real losses. Indirect and consequential damages are excluded entirely, and the cap does not carve out data breaches, which is unusual and worth pushing back on if you're handling sensitive data.

11.1 Except in respect of the exclusions in clause 11.3, each party's total aggregate liability arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, whether in contract, tort (including negligence) or otherwise, shall not exceed the total fees paid or payable by the Customer in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim. 11.2 Neither party shall be liable for any indirect, incidental, special or consequential loss, or loss of profits, revenue, data, or goodwill. 11.3 Nothing in this clause 11 limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud, or any liability which cannot be excluded by law.

You indemnify the Supplier against claims arising from your use of the deliverables — standard, but note it's one-directional in this draft. The Supplier only indemnifies you for IP infringement claims, not for its own negligence or breach generally. Worth asking for a mutual indemnity on ordinary breach.

12.1 The Customer shall indemnify and hold harmless the Supplier from and against any claims, damages, or expenses arising out of the Customer's use of the Deliverables in breach of this Agreement or applicable law. 12.2 The Supplier shall indemnify the Customer against any claim that the Deliverables infringe a third party's intellectual property rights, provided the Customer promptly notifies the Supplier and permits the Supplier to control the defense.

Fairly standard mutual confidentiality obligation, surviving five years after termination. Carve-outs for information that's independently developed, publicly available, or required to be disclosed by law are all present and reasonably drafted.

15.1 Each party shall keep confidential all non-public information disclosed by the other party and shall not use it other than to perform its obligations under this Agreement. 15.2 The obligations in this clause 15 do not apply to information that: (a) is or becomes public through no fault of the receiving party; (b) was lawfully known before disclosure; (c) is independently developed; or (d) must be disclosed by law or court order. 15.3 This clause survives termination of this Agreement for five (5) years.

The Supplier keeps ownership of all deliverables and pre-existing IP, and you only get a license to use them — and that license is non-exclusive, meaning the Supplier can build and sell the same thing to your competitors. If you're paying for bespoke work, you may want ownership, or at least exclusivity in your field.

16.1 All intellectual property rights in the Deliverables, and in any pre-existing materials incorporated into them, shall remain vested in the Supplier (or its licensors). 16.2 The Supplier grants the Customer a non-exclusive, non-transferable, worldwide license to use the Deliverables for the Customer's internal business purposes for the term of this Agreement. 16.3 Nothing in this clause 16 transfers ownership of any Supplier IP to the Customer.

Standard exclusive-jurisdiction clause. Not remarkable unless the chosen forum is inconvenient for you to litigate in — check whether that's the Supplier's home jurisdiction and factor in the practical cost of a dispute there.

21.1 This Agreement and any dispute arising out of or in connection with it shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the jurisdiction specified in Schedule 1. 21.2 The parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of that jurisdiction.
