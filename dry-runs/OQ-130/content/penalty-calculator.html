What clause 14.2 actually says

Clause 14.2 is a liquidated damages provision, not a penalty in the punitive sense — it fixes, in advance, a genuine pre-estimate of the loss caused by late delivery, so neither side has to prove actual damages later. It applies automatically once the delivery date in Schedule 2 passes, accrues per calendar day (not business day), and is capped so a very long delay can't spiral into an unbounded claim.

14.2 LATE DELIVERY. (a) If the Supplier fails to deliver the Services by the Delivery Date specified in Schedule 2, the Supplier shall pay to the Customer, as liquidated damages and not as a penalty, an amount equal to zero point five per cent (0.5%) of the Contract Value for each calendar day (or part thereof) that delivery is delayed, calculated from the day after the Delivery Date. (b) Amounts due under this clause 14.2 shall be deducted from any sums otherwise payable to the Supplier, or invoiced separately at the Customer's election. (c) The Supplier's total liability under this clause 14.2 shall not exceed ten per cent (10%) of the Contract Value, irrespective of the length of the delay. (d) This clause is without prejudice to the Customer's right to terminate for material breach under clause 17.
