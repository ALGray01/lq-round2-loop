QUESTION (OQ-115 — Build a legal-task-aware model router):
Lawyers running multiple coding/agent harnesses route between models by gut — cheap model for mechanical work, frontier model for hard reasoning — and burn through subscription token caps with no system deciding where each request goes. Generic routers (OpenRouter-style) optimise on raw API price and ignore what actually matters for legal work: different legal task types (litigation reasoning, transactional drafting, contract review, legal research, citation-checking) have different best-fit models, and the stakes differ wildly (an internal memo vs. a filing that goes on the record). Build the router that reflects that. In 90 minutes: (1) define the routing policy — map legal TASK TYPES to model/tier choices, layer in a STAKES dimension (higher stakes → most reliable tier plus a verification pass; a court-bound deliverable is the extreme case), and prefer whichever subscription lane still has token headroom — and confront the hard part head-on: legal benchmarks are immature and contested (a 14-17% 'pass rate' barely tells you anything), so state explicitly what signal you actually route on (published legal benchmarks, your own small eval set, task heuristics) and where it is unreliable; (2) ship a WORKING thin router — even a single-provider-plus-fallback proxy or library — that classifies an incoming request by legal task type, picks a model, and logs the routing decision (what/why) so the choice is auditable, not asserted; (3) demonstrate it end-to-end on at least one high-stakes request that escalates to a reliable tier plus a verification pass, and state honestly what your router does NOT yet handle. A runnable router with a defensible, legal-task-aware routing policy beats an abstract routing diagram. Level: L4
